# LLPS Predictor Methods ReportCompiled for: membrane protein LLPS analysis  Date: 2026-06-15\---## OverviewSix predictor tools are used in this analysis, producing 16 score columns. Five are standalone tools (PICNIC, PSAP, PSPHunter, PSPire, and FuzDrop p(LLPS)). The remaining eleven scores come from `PSPspredict\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\_full\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\_proteome.xlsx`, which is **not a published ensemble model** but a research compilation spreadsheet by Wenbin Li (Xiamen University, author of the PDL tool) that aggregates scores from nine other methods and re-runs PSPHunter and PSPire. All sub-scores in that file have been **percentile-ranked across the human proteome** — a score of 0.9 means the protein ranks in the 90th percentile, not that the raw tool output was 0.9.\---## Standalone Predictors### PICNIC / PICNIC (GO)**Full name:** Proteins Involved in CoNdensates In Cells  **Published:** Pulido-Quetglas et al., *Nature Communications* 15, 10668 (2024). DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-55089-x**Method:** Random forest (8 features) using IUPred disorder score, sequence complexity, amino acid co-occurrence patterns, and per-residue AlphaFold2 pLDDT. The GO variant adds 10 Gene Ontology annotation features (most predictive: RNA-binding GO terms), giving 18 features total.**Score:** \[0, 1] classifier probability of being a condensate component. The GO score is systematically inflated for RNA-binding proteins because the top feature is GO annotation — creating circularity for annotated RNA-binders regardless of their actual phase behaviour.**Scale note:** Scores here are raw, not percentile-normalised.**Key bias:** Trained on condensate *membership* (broader than direct LLPS evidence), so it predicts compartmentalisation rather than intrinsic phase separation capacity. The GO score is unreliable for unannotated or newly characterised proteins.\---### PSAP**Full name:** Phase Separation Absolute Probability  **Published:** van Mierlo et al., *Cell Reports* 34, 108705 (2021). DOI: 10.1016/j.celrep.2021.108705**Method:** Random forest (1,200 trees, balanced class weights) trained on amino acid frequencies and biophysical properties alone — no sequence order, structural, or functional annotation features.**Score:** Random forest vote fraction, \[0, 1] in principle, but the proteome-wide range is **\[0, \~0.14]** because true PSPs are rare and the model calibrates against the full proteome composition. A score of 0.05 is therefore high in context. The file suffix `\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\_excluding\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\_training\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\_set` reflects the exclusion of known positives to prevent data leakage; this removes \~145 proteins from the proteome output.**Scale note:** Do **not** compare raw values directly with other tools. Rank-based normalisation is essential.**Key bias:** Captures compositional biases only (high aromatic, high charged residue content). Cannot distinguish condensate drivers from passengers. No sequence-order or structural information — misses ordered-domain-mediated condensation entirely.\---### PSPHunter**Full name:** PSPHunter (Phase-Separating Protein Hunter)  **Published:** Sun et al., *Nature Communications* 15, 2662 (2024). DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-46901-9**Method:** Ensemble ML (SVM, naive Bayes, neural network, random forest, LightGBM, XGBoost) combining sequence features with functional annotations. Uniquely outputs both a protein-level probability and residue-level phase-separation drivers.**Score:** \[0, 1] probability. The `Type` column classifies proteins as:* `PSProteome` — predicted positive* `non-PSProteome` — predicted negative* `Other` — outside training distribution or ambiguous**Scale note:** Raw probability, directly interpretable.**Key bias:** Trained on a relatively small human PSP set (n=167). Residue-level predictions validated mostly on nuclear condensates.\---### PSPire**Full name:** Phase-Separating Protein predictor incorporating both IDR and non-IDR proteins  **Published:** Hou et al., *Nature Communications* 15, 2147 (2024). DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-46445-y**Method:** XGBoost classifier using AlphaFold2-derived structural features, including surface-exposed structured patches (SSUPs: non-IDR residues with RSA > 25%) and phosphorylation frequency alongside IDR content. Explicitly designed to capture PSPs that lack long IDRs — a class missed by most other tools.**Score:** \[0, 1] XGBoost probability. `Include\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\_IDRs` (boolean per protein) flags whether the protein has IDRs above threshold; this lets users distinguish IDR-driven vs. structured-domain-driven predictions. Despite the non-IDR motivation, scores are still heavily right-skewed for membrane proteins (median 0.19 vs. 0.88 in the full experimental set), suggesting membrane topology is a confounding factor.**Scale note:** Raw probability, directly interpretable.**Key bias:** AlphaFold2 dependency — predictions may be unreliable where pLDDT is low. Motivated by non-IDR PSPs but trained data still dominated by canonical condensate families.\---## PSPspredict Compilation (sub-scores)**What it is:** A research spreadsheet (not a published tool) created by Wenbin Li (Xiamen University) aggregating 11 predictor scores across \~20,368 human proteins. All values are **percentile-normalised to \[0, 1]** within the human proteome. The "PredictAll" sheet contains all proteins; "Res985" is a high-confidence subset (≥0.985 on PDL). Repository: https://github.com/xmuzhanglab/PSPsPredict> \\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*Critical note for this analysis:\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\* Because values are percentile ranks, these scores tell you where a protein sits relative to \\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*all human proteins\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*, not its absolute propensity. A membrane protein at PSPspredict\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\_PLAAC = 0.6 ranks in the 60th percentile of the entire proteome for PLAAC — which is meaningful for relative ranking but cannot be compared as an absolute probability against standalone PSAP or PSPHunter scores.\---### PDL**Full name:** Protein Dual-model Language  **Published:** Li et al., *Briefings in Bioinformatics* 26(6): bbaf681 (2025). DOI: 10.1093/bib/bbaf681**Method:** Fuses a ProtT5 large language model encoder (global sequence context) with a KmerConv module (1D convolutions on local k-mer patterns), combined via multi-head attention. The author's own tool in this compilation.**Score in file:** Percentile-normalised \[0, 1] (raw output is \[0, 1] probability, then rank-transformed).\---### catGRANULE**Full name:** Categorical Granule predictor  **Published:** Bolognesi et al., *Cell Reports* 16(1): 222–231 (2016). DOI: 10.1016/j.celrep.2016.05.076**Method:** Linear combination of: (1) structural disorder propensity, (2) nucleic acid-binding propensity (via catRAPID), (3) Arg-Gly/Phe-Gly motif content. Captures RNA-binding granule proteins well.**Score in file:** Percentile-normalised \[0, 1] (raw output is a continuous propensity score, positive values indicate granule formation propensity).**Key bias:** Originally trained on yeast prion data and stress granule RNA-binding proteins. Systematically over-predicts LLPS for nucleic acid-binding proteins.\---### PLAAC**Full name:** Prion-Like Amino Acid Composition  **Published:** Lancaster et al., *Bioinformatics* 30(17): 2501–2502 (2014). DOI: 10.1093/bioinformatics/btu310**Method:** Hidden Markov model trained on yeast prion domain amino acid composition. Identifies the highest-scoring Q/N-rich prion-like subsequence (≥60 aa) and outputs a log-likelihood ratio (COREscore or LLR).**Score in file:** Percentile-normalised \[0, 1] (raw output is a log-likelihood ratio, unbounded).**Key bias:** Only captures Q/N-rich prion-like domains. Misses aromatic/arginine-driven LLPS (e.g., FUS, G3BP1), structured-domain condensates, and most membrane protein contexts. Trained on yeast — applicability to human is indirect.\---### PScore**Full name:** Phase separation Score (pi-contact scoring)  **Published:** Vernon et al., *eLife* 7: e31486 (2018). DOI: 10.7554/eLife.31486**Method:** Quantifies predicted frequency of long-range planar pi-pi contacts between sp2-hybridised groups (Tyr, Phe, Trp, Arg, His, Asn, Gln) within IDRs, using a statistical potential from PDB structures.**Score in file:** Percentile-normalised \[0, 1] (raw output is a continuous score, typical range 0–6+; raw threshold ≥4 indicates high confidence).**Key bias:** Only detects pi-driven LLPS. Performs well for FUS/hnRNP-type proteins but poorly for charge-driven, hydrophobic, or Q/N-rich condensates and completely misses structured-domain-mediated condensation.\---### ESpritz-DisProt**Full name:** ESpritz, DisProt-trained variant  **Published:** Walsh et al., *Bioinformatics* 28(4): 503–509 (2012). DOI: 10.1093/bioinformatics/btr682**Method:** Ensemble of three bidirectional recurrent neural networks (BRNNs), each trained on a different disorder definition. The DisProt variant is trained on manually curated long disordered regions from DisProt. Protein-level score here is likely the mean per-residue disorder probability (i.e., IDR fraction).**Score in file:** Percentile-normalised \[0, 1].> \\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*This is a disorder predictor, not an LLPS predictor.\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\* It is included as a proxy for IDR content. Many disordered proteins do not phase-separate; some ordered proteins do. Use with caution as an LLPS feature.\---### SEG**Full name:** SEG (Sequence segment complexity)  **Published:** Wootton \& Federhen, *Computers and Chemistry* 17(2): 149–163 (1993). DOI: 10.1016/0097-8485(93)85006-X**Method:** Sliding-window algorithm computing local Shannon entropy-based compositional complexity. Windows below a complexity threshold are flagged as low-complexity regions (LCRs). Score here is likely the **fraction of sequence masked by SEG**.**Score in file:** Percentile-normalised \[0, 1].> \\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*SEG was designed for database search filtering, not LLPS prediction.\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\*\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\* It detects compositionally biased sequence regardless of biological function. Many LCR-containing proteins do not phase-separate. Use only as a very crude IDR/LCR proxy.\---### DeepPhase**Full name:** DeePhase  **Published:** Saar et al., *PNAS* 118(15): e2019053118 (2021). DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2019053118**Method:** Combines explicit biophysical feature vectors with protein language model embeddings. Trained specifically on **homotypic** LLPS — proteins that self-assemble without obligate partners. Note: only 30/60 membrane proteins had scores, reflecting lower coverage in the original tool.**Score in file:** Percentile-normalised \[0, 1] (raw output is probability \[0, 1]).**Key bias:** Trained on homotypic condensation only. Systematically under-predicts partner-dependent LLPS clients. Training data biased toward stress granule and P-body components (human/yeast).\---### SaPS-10fea**Full name:** Self-assembling Phase Separation, 10-feature model (from PhaSePred)  **Published:** Chen et al., *PNAS* 119(24): e2115369119 (2022). DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2115369119**Method:** Gradient-boosted classifier using 10 features calculated across the full protein: hydropathy, FCR, IDR percentage, LCR score, PScore, PLAAC, catGRANULE, DeepCoil (coiled-coil), phosphorylation frequency, DeePhase score. Targets **self-assembling** (scaffold/driver) condensate proteins.**Score in file:** Percentile-normalised \[0, 1].**Key bias:** The 10 features are all biased toward Q/N/aromatic IDR-driven condensation. The model inherits these collective blind spots and cannot predict partner-dependent clients.\---### PdPS-10fea**Full name:** Partner-dependent Phase Separation, 10-feature model (from PhaSePred)  **Published:** Chen et al., *PNAS* 119(24): e2115369119 (2022). DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2115369119**Method:** Companion to SaPS in PhaSePred. Trained on the same 10-feature vector but on experimentally verified **partner-dependent** (client) condensate proteins rather than self-assembling scaffolds.**Score in file:** Percentile-normalised \[0, 1].**Key bias:** The self-assembling vs. partner-dependent distinction is noisy experimentally and context-dependent. Inherits all biases of the 10 constituent features. Training set for partner-dependent proteins is substantially smaller.\---## Summary Table|Score column|Full name|Mechanism captured|Scale|Raw or percentile?||-|-|-|-|-||PICNIC\_score|PICNIC|Condensate membership (seq + AF2)|\[0,1]|Raw probability||PICNIC\_GO\_score|PICNIC with GO features|Condensate membership + GO annotations|\[0,1]|Raw probability||PSAP\_score|Phase Separation Absolute Probability|Amino acid composition only|\[0, \~0.14]|Raw (compressed)||PSPHunter\_prob|PSPHunter|Ensemble ML, seq + functional features|\[0,1]|Raw probability||PSPire\_score|PSPire|Seq + AF2 structure, inc. non-IDR PSPs|\[0,1]|Raw probability||PSPspredict\_PDL|Protein Dual-model Language|LLM + KmerConv (general LLPS)|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**||PSPspredict\_catGRANULE|catGRANULE|Disorder + RNA-binding motifs|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**||PSPspredict\_PLAAC|PLAAC|Q/N prion-like domains|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**||PSPspredict\_PScore|PScore|Pi-pi contacts in IDRs|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**||PSPspredict\_ESpritz\_DisProt|ESpritz-DisProt|**Disorder only** (IDR proxy)|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**||PSPspredict\_SEG|SEG|**LCR fraction** (IDR proxy)|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**||PSPspredict\_DeepPhase|DeePhase|Homotypic LLPS (seq + LLM)|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**||PSPspredict\_SaPS\_10fea|SaPS (PhaSePred)|Self-assembling LLPS scaffold|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**||PSPspredict\_PdPS\_10fea|PdPS (PhaSePred)|Partner-dependent LLPS client|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**||PSPspredict\_Psphunter|PSPHunter (re-run)|Same as PSPHunter above|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**||PSPspredict\_PSPire|PSPire (re-run)|Same as PSPire above|\[0,1]|**Percentile rank**|\---## Key Implications for Membrane Protein Analysis1. **Most tools are IDR-centric.** PLAAC, PScore, catGRANULE, SEG, and ESpritz all detect IDR/LCR features that are underrepresented in membrane proteins due to transmembrane domain constraints. Low scores for membrane proteins are expected and may not indicate false negatives.2. **PSPire is the only tool explicitly designed for non-IDR PSPs**, yet membrane proteins still score low (median 0.19 vs. 0.88 in all experimental). This may reflect that PSPire's non-IDR mechanism (surface-exposed structured patches) does not align well with membrane topology.3. **PSAP requires rank transformation** before any cross-predictor comparison — its raw \[0, \~0.14] scale makes it incomparable to \[0,1] probability tools. Despite this, it shows the strongest Spearman correlation with FuzDrop p(LLPS) (ρ=0.59) in the membrane set.4. **PSPspredict sub-scores are already rank-normalised**, so they are internally comparable to each other but their absolute values do not reflect the same calibration as the standalone tools.5. **ESpritz and SEG are disorder/LCR proxies, not LLPS predictors.** Their inclusion in the comparison should be treated as measuring IDR content rather than phase separation propensity directly.6. **PScore, PLAAC and PdPS show the smallest gap between membrane and all-experimental** (PLAAC p=0.037\*, PScore p=0.15 ns, PdPS p=0.019\*) — suggesting these mechanisms (pi-pi contacts and Q/N prion-like domains) are somewhat more evenly distributed between membrane and non-membrane experimental PSPs.
